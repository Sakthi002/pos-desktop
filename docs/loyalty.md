# Points

Off until a shop turns it on, in **Settings → Points**. A shop that does not run
a scheme never sees a points line anywhere.

## The two rates

They answer different questions and move at different speeds.

- **Points per ₹100 spent** — what a bill earns. Counted on the taxable value:
  after discounts, before tax. Tax is not the shop's money and the shop should
  not pay points on it.
- **What a point is worth** — what it takes off a bill when it comes back.

A card keeps the earning rate it was issued with. Raising the shop's rate
rewards new cards; it does not silently rewrite what an old card promised.

## Cards

One card per customer, issued from the Customers screen. The walk-in customer
cannot hold one — points belong to somebody the shop can recognise again.

A card carries a tier (Silver, Gold, Platinum), its own earning rate and an
expiry. A card that has lapsed or been made inactive can neither earn nor be
spent; the balance stands and is still visible.

## The balance

Summed from the transactions, every time it is asked for. There is no stored
total, because a stored total and a list of entries are two answers to one
question and they drift.

Entries cannot be edited or deleted — the database refuses. A mistake is
corrected by another entry, with a reason that stays on the card.

## Spending

Two caps apply, and the lower one wins:

- the balance on the card, and
- the share of the bill the shop allows points to pay for.

Below the shop's floor — the fewest points worth redeeming — nothing is
offered. The caps are worked out against the bill **before** points are spent,
so spending them cannot make room for more.

A redemption is a discount on the bill. The customer pays less, so the taxable
value and the GST on it are lower, which is the position when a discount is
given at the time of supply.

## When goods come back

A void takes back everything that sale earned and returns everything it spent.
A return does the same in proportion to the value that came back: a fifth of
the invoice returns a fifth of the points.

A balance can go negative if points were earned, spent elsewhere, and then the
sale was returned. That is the truth, and it is left visible rather than
quietly floored.

## The books

Points are a liability: the shop owes them. Earning debits **Loyalty points**
(5400) and credits **Loyalty points owed** (2300); spending unwinds both. A
correction made by hand posts its own journal voucher, so what a card says and
what the books say cannot drift apart.

## The receipt

When the shop asks for it, the receipt carries one line: points used, points
earned, and the balance after the bill. It is printed from what was stored with
the sale, so a reprint years later shows what the customer was told at the
counter — not what today's rate would say.
